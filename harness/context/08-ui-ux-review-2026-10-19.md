# CashNarra UI and UX review — 19 October 2026

Requested by the user: reanalyse UI/UX, principles, satisfaction, vision and language. This is an assessment of the current product, not a claim of customer research or launch readiness.

## Evidence and scope

Inspected the live signed-in Today, Money Stories, Plan, goal amount dialog, narration entry, Records and Settings screens. Compared Today with the supplied Narra v2 desktop specimen and implementation brief. Read the relevant component code. Did not save, correct or delete the user's records. Do not copy their financial details into review artifacts.

Git advanced during the session from 694e2ff through Cursor commits to efd6f25. Preserve those commits. The newer calculation review already removes the unnecessary single-basis dropdown and displays the amount and included records; do not recommend the old design as if it were current. Earlier test results apply to the revisions they exercised, not automatically to later commits.

## Verdict

The visual foundation is recognisably Narra: violet/ink/apricot, clear amount hierarchy, a prominent narration panel, quiet record separators and an editorial story treatment. Keep it. The larger gap is interaction quality: users still encounter implementation vocabulary, repetitive cautions, misleading scope labels and flows that require too much interpretation. The experience must consistently answer: what happened, what does it mean, and what can I do next?

## Priority 0 — clarity that affects trust in money

1. **Today inherits the Money Stories period.** Observed: after viewing Monthly and returning to Today, the cards still show the monthly figures under “Money in · selected day” and “Money out · selected day.” Code uses one shared `period` and `r = report(w, from, to)`. Use separate day/report scopes and show the actual date beside the figures. Acceptance: change a report to monthly, return to Today, and assert day totals and day labels match, including clicks into evidence. This is a functional scope bug, not a cosmetic label preference.
2. **Current-month wording presents a future endpoint as established fact.** A report on 19 October says “You owe on 31 October.” Distinguish “October so far · through 19 Oct” from future-dated entries, and keep the ledger facts unchanged. Acceptance: no forecast or future-completeness implication; included records and dates agree with the displayed basis.
3. **Saved status outlives its action.** “Saved · records confirmed” remains across navigation, including an empty narration editor. Show a short action-specific receipt, then remove the global success banner. Draft states belong beside the current draft. Acceptance: a new story cannot appear saved because the last story succeeded; lost-response recovery still works.
4. **Calculation labels must match included kinds.** Current `shortBasis()` says “Sales, including unpaid sales” / “Money received from customers”, while the calculation can also include `income`. Either retain “other income” in the label or narrow the calculation only when explicitly requested. Do not make a short label less true. Acceptance: a fixture with non-sales income displays the correct basis and exclusions.

## Priority 1 — reduce work without hiding important choices

5. **One composer for typing and voice.** Today’s sample-shaped field is a button that opens another screen; voice starts separately. Make the main invitation an actual composer, with Type / Record choices in the same place, and retain words when switching. Show recording capability before the microphone step. Do not ask users to record first and only then discover transcription is unavailable. The API currently needs audio permission; do not fake successful transcription.
6. **Clarifications should be answerable in place.** A missing account should offer account choices, not open a complete record form. A sales total with an unpaid part should ask “Is the unpaid amount included in that total?” only when meaning is uncertain. Keep direct editing as an alternative. Acceptance: resolve the material question without re-entering facts already understood, and preserve all reviewed edits on error.
7. **Goals need explicit add/remove actions.** The current amount field tells users to type a minus sign to remove money. Use “Put towards this goal” and “Take off this goal”, both accepting positive amounts. Show how much is available to mark and how much is already marked elsewhere. Do not imply a bank transfer or protected savings. If a later spending record conflicts with goal marks, offer a clear path to adjust them while keeping the unfinished record intact.
8. **Insufficient goal history needs a next step.** “No useful date yet” and a paragraph about three months explain a limitation without helping. Say “Not enough history to estimate yet” with “Review past months” or “Try a monthly amount”. Keep real-history pace separate from a what-if. Missing months remain unknown.
9. **Money Stories should communicate the user's period.** The current numbered sections largely repeat generic distinctions (“Received does not always mean sold”, “Clear boundaries”). Lead with facts from this period: the change, largest recorded outflows, unpaid sales and what remains owed. Offer the evidence beneath each claim. These can be deterministic summaries; do not introduce a new AI dependency or invent profit/causality.
10. **Records should be easier to scan and retrieve.** Default to clearly grouped recent dates, short human descriptions, aligned amounts and separate payment status. Keep full wording in details. Add explicit date/account filters and a visible reset. Avoid labels such as “Full payment of the receivable” when “Payment from [name]” is available. Source text and history must remain intact.

## Priority 2 — coherence and comfort

11. **Settings structure and orientation.** The Settings breadcrumb currently says “Your story”; the avatar remains “AW” in a different workspace. Use actual workspace initials and page names. Separate appearance, device privacy, story storage, people and account actions. “People, payment dates and goal settings” is an overloaded destination.
12. **Separate deletion from a duration selector.** “Remove text now” appears alongside 7/30 days. A destructive immediate action should be explicit, preview its scope, and allow cancellation. Keep lawful record retention separate from deleting original narration. This is error prevention, not an extra confirmation on ordinary saving.
13. **Use one clear action and quieter secondary actions.** Maintain the existing aligned rows and restrained surfaces. Reduce repeated outlined-button groups, long record titles and the global status stripe. Keep the key question and action visible on mobile; allow longer lists to disclose detail. Retain keyboard focus, 44px targets, amount font and light/dark tokens.
14. **Check-in should feel optional and helpful.** A zero counter plus “A check-in does not mean all activity is captured” feels like system bookkeeping. Offer “Anything else to add?” and optional “I’m done for today”, with catch-up available. Do not equate a check-in with complete finances or add guilt/streak penalties.

## Language standard

Use ordinary Ghanaian-English money language, direct verbs, short sentences and specific dates. Friendly does not mean childish; precision does not require accounting jargon. Put essential consequences beside the action and background explanations under a disclosure. Keep safety in the behaviour, not repeated disclaimers.

| Current language | Better direction |
| --- | --- |
| Inspect outstanding balances | See what’s still owed |
| Trace account balances | See how this balance adds up |
| Draft persistence is on | Keep unfinished stories on this device |
| Record access request | Request a copy of my data |
| No useful date yet | Not enough history to estimate yet |
| Operating expense | Business expense (where business context applies) |
| Included records, formula and exclusions | How we worked this out |
| 2026-10-19 | 19 Oct 2026; Today where appropriate |

Do not simply replace “allocation” with “saved money”: that could falsely imply money moved or was protected. Likewise, retain the distinction between sales, received money, debt and profit.

## Customer satisfaction validation

No satisfaction scores or customer-success claims are justified from an agent review. Run observed sessions with representative users on: first account and unknown starting balance; story with cash/credit sale; correction; percentage with a date range; voice failure/recovery; goal add/remove; finding and explaining a figure. Record completion without help, unnecessary clarification count, time to first correct save, recovery success and a short post-task ease rating. Ask users to explain what a balance and a goal amount mean in their own words. Misunderstanding is a failure even if every button works.

Implement the four trust findings first, then composer/clarification and goal interactions, then report language and visual polish. Use focused regression tests and inspect 320/390/tablet/desktop light/dark. Preserve the agreed stack and ledger. No new banking, tax-advice or money-moving scope is proposed.
