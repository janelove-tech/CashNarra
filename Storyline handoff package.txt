# CashNarra: Brand, UI, UX, and Product Design Guide

Act as a brand strategist, creative director, and senior product designer experienced in conversational AI, financial UX, accessibility, and practical design systems.

Research and create the best coherent branding, UI, and UX direction for CashNarra, then turn it into a **complete brand and product-design guide we can actually build with**.

## 1. Read and reconcile the sources

Preserve:

**Name:** CashNarra 
**Tagline:** Tell Your Money Story. See the Numbers. 
**Product structure:** Record → Understand → Plan 
**Experience principle:** Serious money. Light interaction.

CashNarra is a **conversational financial memory and navigation system**. Users describe ordinary money activity; CashNarra turns it into trustworthy records, useful explanations, and realistic goal forecasts.

Its core experience is:

**Tell → Understand → Clarify what matters → Review/save simply → See the meaning.**

The sources’ ideas about warm financial minimalism, an abstract “Narra Pulse,” or particular gamification mechanics are design proposals to evaluate, not automatically approved visual assets.

Respect any genuinely approved logo, palette, or component system found in the sources. If none exists, make a clear recommendation rather than inventing an existing identity.

### Scope the design must support

- Personal/business workspaces and manually tracked Cash, Bank, and MoMo accounts.
- Text narration first, with a coherent path to short voice recording.
- Multiple financial events extracted from one narration.
- Important clarifications, simple batch review, and conversational corrections.
- Income, expenses, transfers, loans, owner money, receivables/payables, and settlements.
- An accessible ledger with traceable records and totals.
- Daily, weekly, and monthly Money Stories.
- Optional goals, current-pace forecasts, and progressively richer what-if scenarios.
- Forgiving consistency and review indicators.
- Useful exports and privacy/settings controls.

Initial users include people and small businesses in Ghana who find conventional record-keeping cumbersome. Design for differing financial/digital confidence, modest phones, low data usage, and unreliable connectivity. Use Ghanaian relevance thoughtfully without stereotyping or assuming all users behave identically.

CashNarra initially records and explains money. It does not hold funds, operate a wallet, initiate users’ payments, offer credit, recommend securities, or file taxes. Automatic bank/MoMo capture and a full ERP are outside the initial scope.

Do not let the visual language or labels imply those capabilities.

## 6. Design the information architecture and core journeys

Recommend a small clear navigation structure for mobile and desktop. Explain where recording, records, accounts, Money Stories, goals, and settings belong.

Make one contextually useful next action obvious. Explain how the home changes for a new user, unresolved records, a reviewed day, and a returning user catching up.

Keep conversation available without forcing every task through a blank chatbot screen.

Define these journeys step by step:

1. First use and the first useful recorded story.
2. Text narration containing several events.
3. Voice capture, transcript review, and accessible text fallback.
4. Necessary clarification and batch save.
5. Correcting, splitting, moving, or removing an incorrect record.
6. Recording an outstanding debt’s partial/full settlement.
7. Checking a period and opening its Money Story.
8. Creating an optional goal and inspecting its forecast.
9. Exploring a what-if scenario without altering actual records.
10. Offline capture, reconnection, and safe recovery.

For each, specify the user’s intent, screens/actions, key copy, what can be skipped, and where control or safeguards are needed.

Give value early while placing necessary authentication, privacy information, and permissions at appropriate moments.

Separate first-release journeys from future capabilities. Preserve a simple usable product while giving later features a natural home.

## 8. Make financial meaning and AI status visible

Define distinct states for:

**Captured locally → Waiting for processing → Draft understood → Needs clarification → Ready to review → Saving → Saved.**

Show what happens when recording permissions are denied, processing fails, the connection drops during saving, a possible duplicate is found, or a previous record changes.

Never say “Saved” before the authoritative save succeeds. Let users recover without re-entering everything.

Clarify the difference between:

- Draft and confirmed records.
- Money received and revenue.
- Cash outflow and expense.
- Transfers and spending.
- Recorded/declared balances and externally verified balances.
- Checked-in days, reviewed records, and reconciled periods.
- Goal allocations and money actually moved.
- Forecast scenarios and actual financial results.

Avoid treating missing information as zero or an absence of records as proof of no financial activity.

Let important totals reveal the records, period, and assumptions behind them. Avoid unsupported “profit,” confidence percentages, and exact completion dates.

For forecasts, specify pace, assumptions, data period, insufficient-data states, uncertainty wording, and explanations of changes. Allow “I cannot estimate a useful date yet.”

For charts, choose appropriate chart types, labels, units, legends, accessible alternatives, category colours, negative-value treatment, and missing-data presentation. Make summaries understandable before the user studies a chart.
