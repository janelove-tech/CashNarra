# Brag Plan: CashNarra — In My Own Words

## What is this app?
CashNarra lets a small-business owner tell the day's money story in her own words — spoken as a voice note or typed as it happens — then proposes records she checks, completes and saves, and shows what came in, what went out and what's still owed. (Design preview — "Coming soon".)

## The angle
**"In My Own Words."** A new angle on the same truth: the film is told entirely by Ama, in first person. Voice notes are how a lot of people already talk business, so the centrepiece is the voice note itself — she speaks the actual example and her words appear on screen as she says them, with every figure picked out. Then the other way in: "Busy with a customer? I type it as it happens." Both roads end in the same reviewed records. Concept spine: **SAY IT. / OR TYPE IT. / SEE THE NUMBERS.**

## Hook (first 2-3 seconds)
Her portrait at closing; "CLOSING TIME. / IN MY OWN WORDS." Her voice, not a narrator's: "Every evening, before I lock up, I tell my phone how the day went." The app's real voice screen rises into frame — a soft record blip — and she starts talking.

## Key moments (the middle)
- The voice note (18 s): a live transcript typed by her voice, word by word (Whisper word timings), figures in apricot, a REC timer, and a waveform driven by her actual voice. The real voice screen sits beneath as a mini device, labelled as a design preview.
- The transcript lands in the composer as editable text; "OR TYPE IT." — the real composer typed stage by stage.
- "It asks what I left out. Which account paid for delivery?" — her answer "MoMo." lands on the tap; she checks eight records and saves.
- Abena GH₵600 owed → −GH₵200 received → GH₵400 (not a new sale); Kojo GH₵300 still owed; the Money Story lands on the score's big lift: GH₵2,300 in · GH₵930 out · GH₵700 owed.

## Outro / punchline
"MY DAY. / MY WORDS. / MY NUMBERS." over her portrait → brand plate on the final chord: logo, "Tell Your Money Story. See the Numbers.", "Say it or type it — in your own words.", "Coming soon".

## User flow worth showing
Say it (voice note → editable transcript) or type it (composer) → clarify + review 8 records → save → see the meaning. Voice capture is the kit's "Voice interaction preview · future capability"; it is labelled on screen ("Voice capture: the app's design preview · editable transcript") and in the end-card fine print.

## Audio direction
- Role: cinematic score under an intimate first-person voice
- Music: **original cinematic score** (`composition/scripts/synth_score.py`), composed to picture: felt piano motif, five-voice string ensemble, cello ostinato, timpani roll, low booms, riser, 3.2 s synthetic hall. Owned outright — no licence or attribution. (User choice.)
- Music treatment: intro piano descent over low strings → hushed pad + sparse low piano under the voice note (kept out of the speech band) → strings swell at "No forms" (23.3 s) → cello ostinato at 84 bpm under the records (30.4–41 s) → strings + timpani roll + riser into the **big lift at 47.6 s** ("Two thousand three hundred came in") → resolve to F major with a boom at **55.4 s** (brand plate) → ring out to silence by 59.8 s. Voice-carved.
- Music cue guidance: strong cues 47.6 s (Money Story) and 55.4 s (brand plate) — both locked; ostinato beat grid 84 bpm from 30.4 s.
- Audio-reactive treatment: the voice-note waveform is driven by her voice energy; warm glows in "Or type it" and on the brand plate breathe with the score.
- SFX posture: sparse — door click, record start/stop blips, air on the hand-off, typing, four taps, saved chime.
- Voice note sound: band-limited "phone mic" treatment so it reads as a recording, distinct from her narration.
- Restraint rule: nothing competes with her voice; the big moments belong to the score only where she pauses or lands a number.
